Principles that great explainers and documentary creators follow, and why. Use judgment; these are not a checklist.

- Speak to the viewer. This is narration heard by a person, not text read on a page: natural spoken language in the narrator's own voice, with a rhythm that sounds good out loud. Never a list of facts read aloud.
- Numbers the way people say them. Keep only the numbers that matter to the story, and round them the way a person would. Use an exact figure only when the exactness is the point.
- Earn the first seconds. People decide almost immediately whether to keep watching, so open inside the story (a vivid moment, a strange fact, a question) instead of with background or "in this video". Make a promise the rest of the video keeps.
- Explain, don't list. A fact only lands when the viewer understands why it matters. Give the key ideas room: say what happened, then why, then what it means. One idea fully understood beats five ideas mentioned.
- Show the reasoning. How someone figured it out, or why something works, is usually the best part. Walk through the logic step by step so the viewer could explain it to someone else afterwards.
- Keep a thread. Each part should follow from the last ("but", "so", "and that's when") and raise the question the next part answers. If a fact doesn't serve the thread, leave it out.
- Make it human and concrete. Real people, real places, specific numbers, small vivid details. Stakes the viewer can feel.
- Use surprise honestly. Correct a common myth when there is one. Save the strongest reveal for when it hits hardest, and give it a moment. Lead with the story people came for; corrections and nuance make great twists inside it, not a replacement for it.
- Be truthful. Every fact comes from the research. Say when something is uncertain or disputed. Never invent numbers, quotes, names or details to make it more dramatic.
- End with meaning. Answer the question you opened with, then say why it matters, or leave one thought that stays. No sign-offs, no "thanks for watching", no "follow for more".
